# What is next

Things that need the owner, a device or a decision — not more code for its own sake. The first two matter most.

1. **The intro track may not be the company's to use.** The tags embedded in `src/assets/music.mp3` read *title* "牽絲戲 【伴奏】" (the bracketed word means "instrumental") and *artist* "10 10". That looks like a backing track of an existing song, not a piece the company made. Nothing on the site claims the company owns it — the copyright notice lists its origin as an open item and tells visitors not to reuse it — but a public company site should not play someone else's recording without a licence. Either replace it with a track the company owns or has licensed, or confirm who made this one and on what terms, and then say so on `legal/copyright` (the `music` section).
2. **A lawyer should read the legal pages.** Ten long-form documents (`legal/privacy`, `terms`, `cookies`, `accessibility`, `acceptable-use`, `copyright`, `disclaimer`, `complaints`, `retention`, `linking`) describe what the site's own code does, in plain language, and say on every page that they are not legal advice and have not been reviewed. They were checked against the code (storage keys, no cookies, no service worker, no beacons, what the status page requests), not against any law. Open items are flagged on the pages rather than guessed:
   - **Terms → Law and disputes** names no governing law, because the company's registered details are not on the site. Supply them.
   - **Copyright → templates.** The downloadable templates are described as starting points; the conditions for passing them on are not stated. Decide and say.
   - **Acceptable use → machine learning.** Whether the site's text may be used to build or train such systems is undecided; the robots file welcomes every visitor. Decide and say.
   - **Linking → redirects.** Retired addresses redirect in the browser after the site loads, not at the server, so tools that do not run scripts do not follow them. Server-side redirects would fix that.
   - **Privacy → the host.** The notice says the site's code has no analytics and cannot read the host's or the font service's logs. Check the hosting account's own settings (analytics, log retention) so the statement stays true, and update `legal/privacy` and `legal/retention` if anything is switched on.
   - **Licences → GSAP** is used under GreenSock's own "Standard (no charge)" licence, which is not an open-source licence. Confirm that it covers this site.
3. **Real facts for the work pages.** Musebase, the customer-facing product and the internal CRM/data system are described in general terms on purpose. Approved descriptions, screenshots or outcomes can replace them; until then nothing is quoted that cannot be backed up. The word "proprietary" for YK Engine follows the owner's wording — confirm it is the one to keep.
4. **Dates for the timeline.** `company/timeline` is told in phases because no dates are published. If dates are supplied, the scrubber can show them (its track spacing is order, not time, and says so). The legal documents' version tables and the guides also carry no dates for the same reason.
5. **The reference and guide pages are general drafts.** The eight guides in Insights, the handbook, design tokens, stack, quality and accessibility pages, the capabilities, integration and lifecycle pages, the company FAQ, hiring, brand and press pages, and the glossary were written without the owner. They claim nothing about any client or project, and they say they are general, but they are statements in the company's voice: read them and cut or correct whatever does not sound like the company.
6. **Careers.** The page lists no roles and invents none. Add real openings to `src/data/pages/company-careers.js` when they exist (the areas explorer and the contact studio's `?type=careers` already route interest to the contact page).
7. **Accessibility, by hand.** What is automated is clean: axe-core on every page at desktop and phone width, a text-contrast measurement of the colour actually painted (`qa/contrast.mjs`), every control operated, eight widths from 280 to 1920 with no sideways scroll, reduced motion. What is not done: a screen-reader pass (NVDA, VoiceOver), a keyboard-only walk of every demonstration, voice control and switch devices, real phones and tablets, Firefox and Safari, and an independent review. The accessibility statement lists each of these as not done and claims no conformance; keep it true as they are done. Text over the animated backgrounds cannot be measured by any tool.
8. **Real-device performance.** Measure on a mid-range phone with `/engineering`'s live budget and the dev-only PerfDebug HUD. The scene budget (2 active on desktop, 1 on phones, 0 under reduced motion) is the dial to turn if it is not enough.
9. **Per-route metadata.** The site is a client-rendered SPA: titles and descriptions update per route, but crawlers and link previews that do not run JavaScript only see `index.html`. Pre-rendering would fix that, and server-side redirects could ride along with it; the page data is already plain JSON, which makes it straightforward.
10. **Keep the catalogues true.** New interactions go in `src/animation/catalog/inventory.js` and the table in `FOREGROUND.md`; new kit components in `src/data/kit.js`; new kinds of page block in the catalogue on `north/blocks`; new non-prose data keys in `SKIP_KEYS` in `searchEngine.js`. Before merging run `node qa/endings.mjs`, `node qa/website-smoke.mjs`, `node qa/contrast.mjs`, `node qa/accessibility.mjs` and `node qa/foreground-inventory.mjs`.
